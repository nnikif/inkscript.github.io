inkScript Help

The purpose of this application is to turn the screen of your elnk device into an interactive paper for a distraction-free writing. By default, there’s no artificial constraints while doing that, this is normal writing, but with eInk.

The same reason you prefer eInk screen to a LCD screen when reading a book is why you should use elnk when writing, whether it's a blog post, a poem or a novel.

1) Connecting your ebook device to a host.

When you start editing a text, you will see an IP address at the bottom of the screen; get your ebook device connected to the same network, open that IP address in the ebook's web browser and you can start editing the text while looking at the screen of the ebook.

If you can't get your ebook connected to the same wi-fi network your phone is connected to, you need to use your phone's hotspot capability, accessible from the "Settings", to connect your ebook device to the phone's shared internet connection. Make sure your mobile internet provider supports this.

2) A keyboard.

If you're running inkScript on a phone, you need to connect a Bluetooth keyboard to it in order to type the text without distractions. The process depends on a particular keyboard. A desktop application which, obviously, doesn't require any extra keyboard, shares the subscription and text folders with a phone app.

3) Dark mode.

To stop the screen of your phone or a desktop application from distracting you, click an icon at the top right of the editor window, and the screen goes dark. Touch the screen of the phone or do a mouse click to leave the dark mode.

4) Editing the text in inkScript.

inkScript uses touchscreen capabilities provided by an elnk device browser. You can click the screen once in order to move the cursor to a particular position in a text. Double click selects a word, triple click selects a paragraph, four clicks select a complete text. You can also select a number of the words by sliding the finger from the start of a selection to its end.

A selected text can be copied, cut and pasted. You can also use Undo/ Redo and apply italics/bold markdown syntax to a selection. A status bar at the top of the screen shows the number of the words and characters in your text — you can disable this in your "Options".

5) inkScript notebooks.

inkScript helps you organize the texts, so that you don't just use it to make first drafts, but also to keep editing the texts. You can make folders and put your texts there, duplicate and move texts, pin them for easy access, use search across the notebooks.

6) Pro version.

A free version of the application allows for about 50 pages of writing, after which you go into a read-only mode until you purchase a subscription.

There are other features that you get when you purchase a subscription:

— a wide choice of fonts

— a "flow" mode that disables touchscreen editing capabilities and the use of keyboard arrows; you have to use Command+"A/W/S/D" instead

— Typewriter sounds for better immersion.
